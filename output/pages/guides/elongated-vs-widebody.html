Elongated vs Widebody — Shape Comparison

Expert buying advice based on verified measured data — not marketing claims.

Paddle shape is one of the most important decisions when choosing a pickleball paddle. The two main shape options are elongated and widebody, each offering distinct trade-offs between reach, power, and sweet spot size.

Elongated Paddles

Elongated paddles are typically 16.5" tall x 7.5" wide, giving them an elongated profile with a longer reach. This shape is favored by advanced players who prioritize reach on serves and returns, as well as power on drives.

PropertyElongated

Best forPlayers who want reach and power

Our data shows that elongated paddles average about 50-100 RPM more spin than comparable widebody paddles, likely due to the increased swing arc on serves. The increased reach also translates to more aggressive serves and returns.

Widebody Paddles

Widebody paddles are typically 16.0" tall x 8.0" wide, giving them a wider, more forgiving profile. The increased width creates a larger sweet spot, making widebody paddles more forgiving on off-center hits.

Which Should You Choose?

Choose elongated if: You want maximum reach on serves and returns, prefer more power on drives, and are comfortable with a smaller sweet spot. This is the shape used by most advanced tournament players.

Choose widebody if: You value consistency and forgiveness over maximum power, prefer a larger sweet spot, or are still developing your off-center hit correction. Widebody paddles are excellent for intermediate players and those who prioritize control.
