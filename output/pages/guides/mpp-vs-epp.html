MPP vs EPP Foam — What's the Difference?

Expert buying advice based on verified measured data — not marketing claims.

Foam core type is one of the most important decisions when choosing a pickleball paddle. The foam determines the feel of every shot, how long the ball stays on the face, and ultimately how much spin you can generate.

MPP (Microcellular Polypropylene)

MPP foam has a finer, more compact cellular structure than EPP. This creates a softer, more springy feel that many players describe as "crisp" or "bouncy." MPP paddles tend to have longer dwell time — the ball stays on the face slightly longer, giving you more time to generate spin and control placement.

MetricTypical Range
Spin RPM2,400 - 2,750+
Weight range7.9 - 8.4 oz
Dwell timeLonger — more spin potential

Best forPlayers who prioritize spin and control

EPP (Expanded Polypropylene)

EPP foam has larger, more open cells, creating a firmer, more direct feel. Shots feel more "connected" and immediate. EPP paddles have shorter dwell time, which means faster hand speed at the net but slightly less spin potential compared to MPP.

The Verdict

If spin is your priority, choose MPP. If feel and hand speed matter more, choose EPP. For most players, the difference is measurable but not dramatic — both foam types produce paddles that are competitive at the club level. The bigger factors are shape, weight, and surface texture.
