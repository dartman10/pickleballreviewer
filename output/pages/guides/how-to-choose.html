How to Choose a Pickleball Paddle

Expert buying advice based on verified measured data — not marketing claims.

Choosing the right pickleball paddle depends on three factors: your playing style, your physical preferences, and your budget. Based on verified measurements from trusted reviewers, here is a data-driven guide to help you pick the right paddle.

Step 1: Choose Your Foam Type

There are two main foam core types in modern pickleball paddles, and the choice affects everything from feel to spin potential.

PropertyMPP (Microcellular Polypropylene)
FeelSofter, springy, slightly muted
Spin potentialExcellent — grippy surface
Dwell timeLonger — more controlled shots
ExamplesCRBN 3X 16mm (2,750 RPM), Holbrook Fuze Elongated
PropertyEPP (Expanded Polypropylene)
FeelFirmer, more connected, direct
Spin potentialGood — but less than MPP
Dwell timeShorter — faster hands
ExamplesEnhance EPP Turbo Elongated, Selkirk Power Air Invicta
PropertyHoneycomb Core
FeelUnique — stiff face with flexible core
Spin potentialVery high on many models
Dwell timeVariable — depends on construction
ExamplesCRBN 1X 14mm, Vatic Pro Prism Flash 16mm

Step 2: Pick a Shape

  • Elongated (tapered): More reach, higher leverage, higher MOI. Best for players who want extra power on volleys and serves. Measured spin RPM consistently 50-100 RPM higher than standard shapes.
  • Widebody: Larger sweet spot, more stable against off-center hits. Best for players who prioritize control and consistency over maximum power.
  • Hybrid: Blends a wider head with a slightly elongated profile. Best for players who want power without sacrificing too much control.
  • Standard: Balanced feel, lower MOI, faster hand speed at the net.

Step 4: Match Power vs Control

Power and control are not opposites — the right paddle gives you both. Here is how to think about it:

Power Paddles
  • Higher spin RPM (2,400+)
  • Heavier weight (8.1-8.5 oz) for more drive power
  • Wider heads or elongated shape for more leverage
  • Best for: aggressive players who hit through the ball
Control Paddles
  • Lower spin RPM (1,900-2,200)
  • Lighter weight (7.5-7.9 oz) for faster hands
  • Standard or widebody shape for larger sweet spot
  • Best for: patients who set up shots and place with precision
